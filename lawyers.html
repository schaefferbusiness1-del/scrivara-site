What you can request

The medico-legal reports counsel actually need

Drafted by the treating-or-reviewing physician, reviewed and signed by them — never an anonymous report mill.

📋

Independent Medical Exam (IME)

A structured, impartial examination and written opinion on diagnosis, causation, impairment and work status — formatted for the file and for cross-examination.

📝

Expert narrative & causation

A narrative report tying the mechanism of injury to the clinical findings and prognosis, with a clear statement of opinion to a reasonable degree of medical certainty.

🔎

Record review & utilization review

A focused review of the longitudinal record — visits, imaging concordance, outcomes, opioid/MME history — for medical-necessity and reasonableness questions.

FAQ

Questions attorneys ask

How fast will I actually get the report?

Most complete requests — meaning the signed authorization, records and your specific questions are all in the portal — are returned as a signed report within about 24 hours. Complex causation reviews or cases with very large record sets can take longer; the physician will tell you up front if so.

Do you work with both plaintiff and defense counsel?

Yes. The physicians render an independent, impartial opinion based on the records and exam regardless of who retains them. Reports are written to withstand scrutiny from either side.

What specialties are available?

MLS is built around spine, pain management and PM&R — the specialties most often at issue in orthopedic injury, workers' compensation, auto and disability matters. The live directory above shows exactly who is currently participating.

What does it cost?

Each physician sets their own report, deposition and trial fees, shown on their profile and itemized on the invoice. You see the price before you engage. When a report is ready, the requesting attorney can pay securely through the portal; Stripe-confirmed payment unlocks the full signed report.

Will the expert sit for deposition or testify?

Many will — each profile indicates deposition and trial availability and rates. You can confirm appearance terms before you commission the report.

How are records and reports kept secure?

Everything moves through an encrypted, HIPAA-ready portal with audit logging. Attorneys only ever see the report they commissioned — never the physician's clinical charts — and a signed patient authorization (HIPAA §164.508) must be on file before any report is released.
